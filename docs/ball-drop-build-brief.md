# Hidden Ball Drop: Build Team Brief

Welcome aboard. This brief hands you everything explored so far for the group 4 ball drop. It covers what the effect is, how the current design works and why, which ideas were tried and dropped, the measurements, and the safety concerns. It ends with a test plan and the decisions still open. You own the final design and the build from here.

Everything in this document comes from a to-scale 3D physics sandbox of the gym layout. The numbers are simulation results, not gym measurements. Treat them as a strong starting point that the gym tests must confirm (see [Test plan](#9-test-plan)).

---

## 1. The effect in one paragraph

A group walks into the third lane (group 4) and sees only black walls. Once they have walked past the front of the lane, a hidden operator pulls a cord. A black drape high on the wall behind them drops away. A giant 6.5 ft inflatable soccer ball rolls out over the top of the 8 ft wall, drops into the lane behind the group, and rolls down the lane after them, like the boulder in an adventure movie. The ball stops on its own at the dead end by the stage. The crew resets it between groups.

**Watch it first:** the sandbox video (29 s, three camera angles) shows the whole sequence: the booth from behind, a kid's eye view from the lane, and a wide view of the run-out. Ask the project lead for the MP4 or GIF, or run the sandbox yourself ([section 11](#11-using-the-sandbox)).

## 2. Where it is in the gym

### Coordinates used in this brief

All positions are in feet, measured on the floor plan:

- **x** runs from the left wall (0) to the right wall (85).
- **y** runs from the top wall (0) down to the stage wall (60).
- **height** is measured up from the gym floor.

The gym is 85 x 60 ft. All maze walls are 8 ft tall black sheeting on a pine frame, 0.5 ft thick.

### The relevant pieces of the layout

| Feature | Where |
|---|---|
| Diagonal wall | Runs from (20, 36) up to (65, 9). It is 52.5 ft long, made of 7 panels of 7.5 ft each. The booth is behind this wall, on the side away from the stage. |
| Group 4 lane (the "third lane") | x 43 to 55 (12 ft wide). It runs from the diagonal wall down to the pony wall at y 56. |
| Entrance into group 4 (from group 3) | Opening at x 43, y 22.2 to 29.85, top left of the lane. |
| Exit from group 4 (to group 5) | Opening at x 55, y 47.75 to 56, bottom right of the lane. |
| Visitor route through group 4 | In at about (43, 26), turn at (49, 26), straight down the middle of the lane to (49, 52), then out right. |
| Pony wall and stage | The pony wall (4 ft) closes the bottom of the lane at y 56. The stage behind it is 4 ft deep and 3 ft high. |

### Where the rig goes

- **Drop point:** the center of the ball opening is on the diagonal wall at x 46.5, y 20.1. Measured along the diagonal wall from its left end (20, 36), that is **30.9 ft**, near the joint between panels 4 and 5.
- **Opening:** 8 ft wide, so it spans **26.9 to 34.9 ft** along the wall from the left end. The booth is 11'-4" wide (25.2 to 36.6 ft).
- **Booth:** stands in the off-route triangle behind the diagonal wall, between the wall and the top of the gym. Visitors can never reach this area. It stands on the floor behind the wall, its front face against the wall's back face, and extends 11'-3" back from there.
- **Landing spot:** about **(49.8, 25.6)**, 5.5 ft into the lane. This is right on the route's turn corner at (49, 26), where visitors start down the lane.

> **Key implication:** the ball lands exactly where visitors walk. Everything about operating this safely follows from that (see [Safety](#8-safety)).

## 3. How the current design works

The rig has six parts. One operator pulls one cord, and everything else follows.

```
  Behind the wall (booth)                     |  Lane (visitor side)
                                              |
   operator     ball resting      stop bar    |  drape on kabuki pins
   platform  -> against bar  -> (pinned up) --+-> covers the opening
   (5 ft)       on a 12° ramp                 |
                                              |
   One T-handle pulls line K (kabuki pins) and line B (stop bar latches)
```

### 3.1 Booth

- **Construction:** a 2x4 frame covered in the same black sheeting as the maze walls.
- **Size:** 11'-4" wide, 11'-3" deep (outside), 17 ft tall. It is a freestanding box: nothing bears on the maze wall.
- **Front:** stands on top of the diagonal wall. Two 1 ft jambs and a header frame an opening **8 ft wide, from the wall top (8 ft) up to 16.25 ft**.
- **Sides and back:** run floor to top. The back has an operator door. The side panels butt tightly against the front panels; a gap at those corners let visitors see in during the sight-line tests.
- **Clearance:** the opening clears the ball by 9 in each side and about 1.3 ft overhead as it rolls out.

### 3.2 Ramp deck

- **Material:** 3/4 in plywood on a 2x4 frame, 8 ft wide, square to the wall, sloped **12 degrees** down toward the lane.
- **Heights:** the deck surface is 9.84 ft at its high end (7 ft behind the wall centerline), 8.30 ft at the lane face of the wall, and 8.09 ft at the lip.
- **Lip:** overhangs the lane face by 1 ft. It must be **rounded and carpeted** so the vinyl ball cannot snag or puncture.
- **Length:** 9'-11 5/8" along the slope, running back to a 2x4 back stop that holds the ball while it is reloaded. Use three sheets of plywood.
- **Support:** 2x4 joists at 16 in on center run down the slope. They bear on the booth front wall's beveled top plate, just behind the maze wall, and on two doubled 2x6 beams on 4x4 posts, 7'-6" apart. Only the doubled-plywood lip cantilevers 1'-6" over the wall, clearing the wall cap by about 2 in. No load goes onto the maze wall.
- **Finish:** paint the deck, lip, and rails **flat black**. From the lane, the lip is the only part of the rig a visitor can see after the drape drops.

### 3.3 Guide rails

- 3/4 in plywood, 18 in tall, with a foam-padded 2x4 cap, along both sides of the deck, 6'-10 1/4" apart inside.
- **Why so tall:** an early version had low 2x6 rails. They only touch the bottom curve of the ball, so an off-center ball rode up them and clipped the opening's frame. 18 in rails push it back to center before it reaches the opening; worst case it runs within about 0.7 ft of center, inside the 0.75 ft side clearance.

### 3.4 Trigger: hinged stop bar on gravity latches

- **Bar:** a 2x4 on edge (3.5 in tall), 6.8 ft long, across the deck between the rails. It is fixed to the deck with two butt hinges along its bottom edge, so it can fold flat toward the lane.
- **Latches:** it stands upright under two steel gravity gate latches, one on each rail's inner face. A reset cord (line R) stands the bar back up from the platform, and the latches click over it on their own.
- **Ball position:** the ball rests against the bar, with its center 5.8 ft behind the wall centerline (along the slope). The bar is 4.4 ft behind the centerline.
- **Load:** on a 12 degree slope, a 12 lb ball pushes on the bar with only about **2.5 lb**. The latches see very little load, so the pull takes little effort.
- **On the pull:** line B lifts both latch arms, and the ball's weight folds the bar flat in about 1.2 s. The ball rolls over the flat bar, which is only 1.5 in thick.

### 3.5 Kabuki drop (the reveal)

- **Drape:** black velour, **10'-10" wide x 8'-9" tall**, filling the width between the returns. It hangs from a 2x4 batten flown on two lift lines from outriggers on the booth's corner posts, **1.4 ft out from the wall centerline**. That clears the ramp lip, so the drape can fall straight down without hitting the deck.
- **Coverage:** it laps the opening by 1.5 ft each side. Its hem hangs at 7.75 ft, a few inches below the lip, so nobody in the lane can see up behind it.
- **Returns:** black panels that continue the booth sides out over the lane, 1'-9" deep, from the drape hem to the booth top, notched over the wall cap. They close the gaps at the drape's edges. Flat black lip fillers close the floor of that pocket beside the ramp. Both were needed: without them, slanted views from the second lane and from the group 3 doorway could see in.
- **Kabuki pins:** six 1/4 in smooth steel pins, each through two screw eyes on the batten and a webbing loop on the drape. All point the same way and tie to line K, so a 3 in pull frees every loop at once. Line B to the stop bar has 2 in of extra slack, so the drape always goes first.
- **Drop timing:** the hem reaches the floor in about **0.7 s**, and the drape is heaped at the wall base by about **1.0 s**. The ball does not reach the drape line until about 2.6 s.

### 3.6 Operator platform

- 4'-0" x 2'-5 1/2", top at 5 ft, against the booth back wall and behind the deck's high end, with a 42 in guardrail. Reached by a ladder through a door in the left side wall.
- The operator stands here to pull the cord, watch the lane monitor, and reload.

### 3.7 Run-out: no tether

- Nothing is tied to the ball.
- The third lane is a **dead end at the stage**, so the ball rolls down after the group and comes to rest near the pony wall.
- In every simulated run, it never went back out the entrance and never continued into group 5.

### 3.8 Reset

1. Between groups, a crew member walks the ball back up the lane.
2. The operator drops a **hoist line** from a pulley over the opening, the crew member clips it to the ball's net, and the operator hauls the ball up onto the deck.
3. Unclip the line.
4. Pull line R to stand the stop bar up; the latches click over it. Ease the ball onto the bar and trip the snap shackle to free the hoist line.
5. Lower the batten to about 6 ft over the lane, hook the drape's loops on the pins, and fly it back up.

**Nothing hangs in the lane while a group is inside.**

## 4. Timeline of one release (simulation)

| Time after the pull | What happens |
|---|---|
| 0.0 s | Pins come out. The drape starts to fall, and the stop bar starts to tip. |
| 0.7 s | Drape hem hits the lane floor. |
| 1.0 s | Drape fully heaped at the wall base. The opening is clear. |
| 1.2 s | Stop bar is flat. The ball is rolling. |
| 2.6 s | Ball reaches the drape line (just past the lip). |
| 2.8 s | Ball leaves the lip at about 7 ft/s. |
| 3.5 s | Ball lands about 5.5 ft into the lane at (49.8, 25.6), coming down at about **19 ft/s (13 mph)**. |
| 3.5 to 4 s | It bounces, then rolls down the lane at about 6 ft/s at first, slowing to about 2 ft/s. |
| about 10 s | About 16 ft past the landing spot (y about 42). |
| about 30 s | Comes to rest near the stage end, around (46.5, 49). |

### How it varies

These runs show how sensitive the drop is to the things you can't control exactly:

| Variation | Lands at | Landing time | Where it stops |
|---|---|---|---|
| Baseline (12 lb ball) | (49.8, 25.6) | 3.45 s | y 49.3 |
| Light ball (6 lb) | (49.6, 25.2) | 3.78 s | y 41.6 |
| Heavy ball (18 lb) | (49.9, 25.8) | 3.50 s | y 52.5 |
| Bouncier (0.75) | (49.8, 25.6) | 3.63 s | y 45.6 |
| Slicker floor | (49.8, 25.6) | 3.52 s | still rolling slowly at 60 s, y 37.8 |
| Held 1 ft off center | front of lane | similar | stays in group 4 |

**Takeaways:**

- The **landing spot is very consistent**, within about 0.6 ft across all variations.
- **Where the ball stops varies a lot** (from mid-lane to the stage end). Plan staffing around the whole lower half of the lane, not one spot.

## 5. Why this design: what was tried and dropped

This is the history of the idea, so you don't repeat dead ends.

1. **Drop from above (first sandbox demo).** The ball fell from 22 ft straight down into the lane after a delay. It proved the ball could fit and bounce in the lane, but it wasn't a build: there was nothing to hold the ball up there or hide it.

2. **Version 1 rig: strip curtain, vanishing stop bar, 25 ft tether.**
   - **Strip curtain:** the ball was supposed to push through black plastic strips. Rejected because the strips' resistance is unknown, and there was no clear mechanism to get the ball through.
   - **Tether:** a cord from the ball's net to a pulley over the opening, to limit run-out and haul the ball back for reset. Rejected because **a rope tied to a rolling ball winds up around it**, and it also puts a rope in the lane with kids.
   - **Stop bar:** it simply vanished on release in the model, which did not show how a real trigger works.

3. **Spring-hinged "saloon" doors the ball pushes open.** Two 4.5 ft black leaves on spring hinges, opening outward over the lane. **In the simulation the ball jammed at the lip every time.** The leaves are long enough that their tips keep pushing back on the ball until it is already rolling off the lip, where it has almost no push left. Damping only on the closing stroke didn't fix it. Doors with no springs did let the ball through, but slowed it (deck speed 5.1 vs 6.5 ft/s) and left the booth open. Rejected.

4. **Current design: kabuki drop, hinged stop bar on latches, free run-out.** Every part has a physical mechanism. One pull triggers it. Nothing stands in the ball's way once the drape is down.

### Ideas considered and not pursued

These are worth knowing as backups:

| Idea | Why not (yet) |
|---|---|
| Roll-up or guillotine panel that lifts out of the way | Not enough headroom. The booth would need to be about 25 ft tall. |
| Drop-down "drawbridge" flap hinged at the wall top | It swings out over the lane in an 8 ft arc, which is a strike hazard. |
| Electromagnetic lock (maglock) instead of gravity latches | Viable upgrade: push-button release, fail-safe on power loss. It needs power at the booth and a design for what happens if power drops mid-show. |
| Automatic trigger (beam break or motion sensor) | Not recommended as the only trigger, because the landing spot is on the route. It could light a "ready" lamp for the operator instead. |
| Catch net, carpet brake strip, or soft bumper in the lower lane | Not needed in simulation, because the dead end stops the ball. Keep them in mind if the real ball rolls too far or too fast, or reaches the exit. |
| Black strip or velour curtain the ball pushes through | Resistance is untested. If you want to try it, test it on the ground first; the simulation can't predict fabric drag. |

## 6. Design changes made because of testing in the model

These came out of the simulation and sight-line checks. Keep them unless you re-test:

- **Rails raised to 18 in, and the opening widened to 8 ft.** Off-center balls clipped the frame with low rails.
- **Opening top raised to 16.25 ft.** The first version cleared the ball by only 1.5 in overhead.
- **Booth sides butted tight against the front.** A small gap let visitors see in.
- **Drape moved out past the lip** (1.4 ft from the wall centerline). Otherwise the deck blocks it from falling.
- **Drape widened to 11 ft, and returns added.** Slanted views from the second lane got past narrower coverage.
- **Drape hem set below the lip.** That blocks the view up into the booth from below.

## 7. How "hidden" was verified

- **Method:** the sandbox casts sight lines from every point a visitor can stand along the route. It checks every 1 ft along the route, at 5 offsets across its width, at **kid (3.5 ft), adult (5.5 ft), and tall adult (6.5 ft)** eye heights. Each line runs to the ball's center and to 5 points on its surface.
- **Result:** with the rig armed, **no line reaches the ball**. A wider scratch check over every reachable spot in the house, on a 1 ft grid, agrees.
- **Control:** the same check does see the ball once it has dropped, so the test isn't simply blind.
- **Limits:**
  - The check treats the drape as an opaque solid. Real velour can sag, gap at its edges, or let light through. **Do the walkthrough check in the gym** with show lighting.
  - Light leaks matter as well as sight lines: keep the booth dark and confirm no work light or monitor glow shows around the drape.
  - Sound gives things away too. Hinges, the bar folding, and the drape dropping all make noise. Use soundtrack cover if needed.

## 8. Safety

This rig drops a 12 lb ball the size of a small car onto the visitor route, from a 17 ft booth that a person stands in. Take these seriously.

### Landing zone (the biggest risk)

- The ball comes down at about **13 mph** on the route's turn corner, roughly (49.8, 25.6).
- **Release rule: only pull when the landing zone is clear.** Keep about a 5 ft radius around the landing spot clear. The group must be well down the lane (about y 35 or beyond, at least 10 ft past the landing spot), and **the next group must not be entering** from group 3. The entrance is just a few feet from the landing spot.
- The operator needs a reliable view of the lane: a small camera with a monitor on the platform, or a peephole in the booth side. Decide this early; it drives the trigger design.
- If anything is unclear (a straggler, a child who turned back, a staff member in the lane), **don't pull**. Skipping the effect for one group is fine.

### Ball rolling after the group

- After landing, the ball rolls at about 6 ft/s at first, slowing to about 2 ft/s, which is faster than a slow walk.
- A soft 12 lb ball is low energy, but at 6.5 ft tall it can knock over a small child.
- **Recommended:** a staff member in the lower lane (a "ball wrangler" in black) who can stop or steer the ball and keep kids moving toward the exit.

### Operator and booth

- **Platform:** add a guardrail. The operator works at 5 ft beside a 12 degree deck with a ball on it.
- **Tipping:** a 17 ft booth on a small footprint can tip. Brace it to the wall framing or the floor, or ballast the base. Check this with whoever owns the maze walls.
- **Wall load:** don't let the ramp's low end load the 8 ft wall panels unless someone confirms they can carry it. The plywood deck and frame alone weigh roughly 150 to 200 lb. Consider dedicated posts just behind the wall.

### Fabric and fire

- Use **inherently flame-retardant velour** (look for NFPA 701 certification) for the drape and any booth fabric. The fire marshal will likely ask.
- Keep the path to the booth's operator door clear.

### Trip and pinch points

- **Fallen drape:** it heaps at the wall base about 1.2 ft into the lane. That is after the group has passed, but **it must be cleared or re-hung before the next group enters**.
- **Stop bar hinge:** keep hands clear of the bar when re-pinning with the ball loaded. Load the ball only after the bar is pinned.

### The ball itself

- Prefer a sealed ball over a constant-air one (no blower noise or hose). Check its pressure each night.
- **Puncture:** no exposed screws, staples, or splinters anywhere the ball touches: deck, rails, lip, and booth edges. Carpet the lip.

### Stop and abort

- **There is no way to stop the ball once released.** The safety plan is the release rule, the lane view, and the ball wrangler.
- Agree on radio calls: "lane clear", "hold", "ball out".

## 9. Test plan

Build and test in stages, and compare each stage to the simulation. If a result differs a lot from the simulation, update the scene file and rerun the tests ([section 11](#11-using-the-sandbox)) before moving on.

| Stage | What to do | Pass if |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Measure the ball | Weigh it, measure its diameter, and record its pressure. Do a rough bounce test: drop it from 8 ft and measure the rebound height. | You have real numbers to put in the scene. The simulation assumes 12 lb, 6.5 ft, and bounce 0.6. |
| 2. Ramp on the ground | Build the deck, rails, and stop bar flat on the floor at 12 degrees (full height not needed). Load the ball, pull line B, and time it. | Latches lift easily with one hand. The bar folds flat in under about 1.5 s. The ball rolls straight and doesn't climb the rails. |
| 3. Drape drop | Hang the drape at full height on its batten with the kabuki pins, and pull. Film it. | It falls straight with no snagging. The hem is down in under 1.0 s (simulation: 0.7 s). All pins release every time. |
| 4. Full rig, no visitors | Assemble at full height and release 10 times. Tape-mark the landing spot and where the ball stops. Time each stage. | Lands within about 1.5 ft of (49.8, 25.6), about 3.5 s after the pull. Never hits the jambs or header. Stays in group 4. |
| 5. Sight-line walkthrough | With the rig armed and show lighting on, walk the whole route at kid, adult, and tall adult eye height (crouch for kids). | No ball, booth interior, or light visible anywhere. |
| 6. Sound check | Release with the soundtrack running. | No telltale noise before the reveal. |
| 7. Reset drill | Time the full reset with the real crew. | It fits within the gap between groups (confirm the group interval). |
| 8. Dress rehearsal | Run volunteer groups with the radio calls and the release rule. | The operator only pulls with the zone clear, and the wrangler handles the run-out. |

### Known limits of the simulation

Remember these when the real thing behaves differently:

- **Ball:** modeled as a rigid sphere. A real inflatable squashes, so expect less bounce and a shorter run-out.
- **Rolling resistance:** approximated, and the gym floor's real value is unknown.
- **Drape:** falls with no air drag; real velour falls a bit slower. There is about 1.5 s of margin before the ball arrives.
- **Contacts and hinges:** hinges have no friction, and contact friction values are estimates.
- **Lip:** modeled as a sharp edge; the real one is rounded and carpeted.
- **Fabric:** the drape is modeled as a solid sheet, so it can't model sag, gaps, or see-through.

## 11. Using the sandbox

The model lives in the project repository (GitHub: `tomalterman/Haunted-House-Layout`). The rig is in pull request #10 until it merges into `main`.

### Run it

```sh
npm run serve        # serves the site on http://localhost:8000
```

1. Open the page and switch to **3D**.
2. The scene **Hidden ball drop (group 4)** loads by default.
3. Press **Release** to pull the cord, and **Replay** to reset.
4. Fly around: drag to look, W A S D to move, E and Q to go up and down, Shift for speed.

### Change it

- **Where things are defined:** all rig geometry is in `src/scenes/ball-drop-rig.js`. It is placed relative to the diagonal wall, so it follows layout changes.
- **What you can change:**
  - Drop point: `STATION_X`
  - Ramp slope: `RAMP_SLOPE`
  - Opening size: `OPEN_W` and `OPEN_TOP`
  - Rails: `RAIL_H` and `RAIL_AT`
  - Drape: `DRAPE_*`
  - The ball's `mass`, `bounce`, and `rollingResistance`
- **Gym measurements:** the layout itself is in `src/layout.js`. Correct a measurement there and everything follows.
- **Check a change:** run `npm test` after any change. It rechecks the following:
  - The ball is hidden from every route point.
  - One pull drops the drape and frees the bar.
  - The ball lands in the front of the lane and clears the opening's frame.
  - It never crosses a wall, and it stays in group 4.
  - All of this holds across ball weight, bounce, floor, and off-center variations.

### Build drawings

`docs/drawings/` holds an 8-sheet drawing set (17 x 11 in): site plan, section, elevations, plan, mechanism details, rigging and release sequence, framing and cut list, and operating notes. Open `ball-drop-drawings.pdf` to print, or the SVG sheets one at a time. `node scripts/drawings.mjs` regenerates the SVG and HTML sheets from the scene file, so the drawings follow any change to the model.

### Build notes in the code

The header of `src/scenes/ball-drop-rig.js` repeats the build notes in this brief next to the numbers, so the two stay together.
